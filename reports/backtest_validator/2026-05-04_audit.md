### 3. No walk-forward validation (Check 9) — 100% in-sample

`backtest_with_charges.py` runs single-pass on full Feb-Apr data with no train/test split. Parameters were "validated" on the same data they were tuned on. This is the **cardinal sin of quantitative trading**.

## Other failures

- **Same-bar-close look-ahead:** Strategies enter at bar close; live execution would happen at next bar's open with 0.5-2% slippage hit.
- **Charges undermodeled:** Missing GST on brokerage (18%), DP charges on delivery (~₹13.5+GST per scrip), stamp duty separation.
- **Single regime (45 days):** Untested in volatile, gap-heavy, or bear-trending markets.
- **Hard-coded parameters:** 9 EMA, 21 EMA, 50 SMA, 200 SMA, BB(20,2σ) — all textbook round numbers, no sensitivity testing or justification comments.

## What owner must do before live ₹2k

A. Re-run backtest with ≥12 months of data (not 45 days)
B. Walk-forward validation: rolling 70/30 train/test windows
C. Next-bar-open execution model (not same-bar close)
D. Full Indian retail cost model: brokerage + STT + GST + DP + stamp + SEBI
E. Multi-regime testing: high-vol, low-vol, trending, range
F. Sample size enforcement: n≥100 per strategy or strategy is dropped
G. Parameter sensitivity: test ±10% around each hard-coded number

## Honest hedge — what couldn't be verified from code alone

2. Actual NSE slippage rates during Feb-Apr 2026.
3. Volatility regime classification of Feb-Apr 2026 — could be a quiet bull period that flatters trend-following + mean-reversion.
4. Whether the 20-stock backtest universe is representative of full Nifty 500 performance.
